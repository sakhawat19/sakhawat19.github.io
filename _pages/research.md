My research connects reliable model evaluation with secure and efficient deployment.

## Reliability: hallucination detection

I investigate how internal model representations and multimodal consistency can identify unreliable outputs. My work on **Late Internal Representations (LIR)** examines hallucination detection using signals from later model layers. Collaborative work on a **modality-agnostic definition of multimodal hallucination** provides a foundation for evaluating errors across modalities.

**Publications:** ICMLA 2025; IEEE AIIoT 2026.

## Security: AI-mediated communication

My ongoing **CloseShell** research investigates whether a reply was written by a human or generated by an AI assistant. The approach combines textual evidence, consistency with the author's previous writing, interaction behavior, and active challenges. I evaluate generalization across authors and generating models.

## Efficiency: model deployment at the edge

My **MMEC** work studies efficient use of Mixture-of-Experts models in mobile edge computing. This direction explores resource-aware inference, quantization, and selective computation to support practical deployment under computational constraints.

**Publication:** ICTC 2025.

## Autonomous agents

I am exploring the behavior and security of persistent autonomous agents, including how collaboration, specialization, tool use, and resource allocation evolve during extended interactions. This is an ongoing research direction.

## Interdisciplinary and distributed systems research

My collaborations include social network analysis of Magdalenian hunter-gatherer societies and LLM-based analysis of annual reports for stock prediction. Earlier work investigated sensor-driven traffic control using wireless sensor networks and distributed fog computing.
